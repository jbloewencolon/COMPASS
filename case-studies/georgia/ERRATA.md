## E1-01 — Title VI: separate legal bases, scope and enforcement

**Status:** unverified proposed correction; agency review pending.

**Observed passage.** S01, `START-HERE.docx`, glossary paragraph beginning
“Disparate impact,” says a neutral rule with unequal effects “is unlawful under
Title VI regardless of whether anyone intended it” and treats funding as a broad
trigger. S02, original anchor workbook sheet3 row31, carries similar language
about disparate-impact regulations and federally funded agency systems. Related
Title VI language-access and disparity assertions flow into S03–S07 and S09–S16.
Those dependencies need review; a mention of Title VI alone is not a finding that
every dependent sentence is wrong.

**Proposed working correction.** Do not reuse a universal statement that every
disparate outcome is unlawful under Title VI, or that any receipt of federal
money establishes every asserted duty for every agency action. Separate the
statute's basis, agency-specific regulations and their current status, recipient
and program/action scope, language-access requirements, administrative/grant
enforcement, and the availability of a private cause of action. Analyze Title VII
or other laws on their own terms rather than merging them with Title VI.

**Primary leads requiring inspection.** Title VI, including 42 U.S.C. §§2000d and
2000d-1; *Alexander v. Sandoval*, 532 U.S. 275 (2001); and the user-reported DOJ
final rule at [90 FR 57141](https://www.federalregister.gov/citation/90-FR-57141).
The rule's reported final status, operative text, effect, dates, and any judicial
developments have not been independently verified here. Do not imply that one
DOJ change automatically determines every funding agency's rules.

**Agency action.** Identify the program, funding agency, recipient, challenged
action, asserted obligation and enforcement route. Obtain the current applicable
primary texts and record agency review before promoting a replacement legal
statement to verified status. Reassess affected F08/F28 and AMD-2026-08/13 reasoning
where it relies on these claims, while keeping historical decisions and proposals
unchanged in the originals.

## E1-02 — HHS Section 504: add a separate scoped date check

**Status:** user-supplied lead, unverified; agency review pending.

**Observed gap.** S06/S07 group “ADA Title II and Section 504” under a DOJ web-rule
discussion. S05 names Section 504 but does not provide a distinct HHS recipient
web/mobile compliance-date analysis. S02–S04 and the hard-date queue in S08 do not
establish a separately verified HHS Section 504 deadline. The ADA-based schedules
and worked accessibility examples in S09–S13, S17 and S18 are dependent review
material, not evidence that HHS scope and dates were checked.

**Proposed working correction.** Keep DOJ ADA Title II public-entity requirements
separate from HHS Section 504 requirements for covered recipients of HHS federal
financial assistance. Add a dated verification task for the user-reported
**11 May 2027** web/mobile compliance date for scoped recipients with **15 or more
employees**, rather than substituting it for an ADA deadline or applying it to
every state agency. Verify the employee-count rule, recipient and program scope,
exceptions, applicable text and current legal status before using the date as an
operative deadline. No deadline has been changed in an original schedule.

**Primary leads requiring inspection.** Section 504, 29 U.S.C. §794, HHS 45 C.F.R.
part 84, and the web/mobile provision at
[45 C.F.R. §84.84](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-84/subpart-I/section-84.84),
with the relevant final/amending rule and current status. The quoted date and
threshold are leads supplied in the review, not an independently verified holding
or deadline in this repository.

**Agency action.** Confirm whether the recipient and activity fall within HHS's
rule, obtain the authoritative current text, and record any separate applicable
ADA/HHS dates in a new working register. Unknown applicability stays unknown.

## E1-03 — EO 14365 §8(b): do not infer a blanket exemption

**Status:** contested scope; primary-source and agency legal review pending.

**Observed passage.** S02/S03 anchor rows, S04, both S06/S07 Prompt 01 variants,
and S05's baseline/source list say stated carve-outs include state government
procurement and use of AI and infer that the preemption debate concerns private
actors rather than the state's own enterprise governance. These historical
statements are not current legal verification.

**Proposed working correction.** Treat the scope and effect of §8(b) as a specific
question to resolve against the exact order. Do not extrapolate a carve-out in
one provision into a categorical exemption from every provision or consequence
of the order, related federal action, enacted law, funding condition or litigation.
Nor should the existence of an order be presented as an enacted preemption
statute or an adjudication. Record the particular provision/action and uncertainty.

**Primary leads requiring inspection.**
[Executive Order 14365](https://www.federalregister.gov/executive-order/14365),
especially §8(b), and any relevant later official action and judicial disposition.
Neither the scope conclusion nor the historical “no challenge confirmed filed”
statement is verified as current here.

**Agency action.** The affected agency appoints the appropriate review lead/legal
reviewer. Until that review is recorded, carry this as contested/unknown in any
new working analysis; do not manufacture a central lead counsel or a settled
preemption conclusion. Later court/AG handling is a separate milestone; this
sidecar does not implement an automated court-effect classification.

## E2-01 — SB-01: investigate scoped U.S. contestability foundations

**Status:** proposed standing-brief replacement; primary verification and agency
policy review pending.

**Observed passage.** S08's standing-brief table asks how other jurisdictions
have drafted contest/appeal/human-redetermination rights. Its Appendix A says
“No jurisdiction has been identified with binding text on contestability that
GTA could adapt.” That describes a historical search result, not proof that U.S.
program-specific review rights are absent. S05 itself already identifies
constitutional procedural due process and Georgia's Administrative Procedure Act
as review material; it must not be ignored when interpreting SB-01.

**Proposed standing brief.** For each in-scope automated or AI-assisted agency
action, identify the existing U.S. source of any notice, opportunity to be heard,
fair-hearing, review or appeal requirement. Record the protected property/benefit
interest where relevant, program and action covered, trigger, timing, decision-maker,
remedy, exceptions and exact primary text. Then ask what policy/control gap remains.
Do not assert that due-process protections or identical hearing rights apply
whenever a program receives federal funding.

**Primary research leads, not verified conclusions:**

- Constitutional procedural due process, assessed against the particular protected
  interest and action; *Goldberg v. Kelly*, 397 U.S. 254 (1970), and *Mathews v.
  Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976), are candidate authorities to inspect, not universal
  AI appeal-right templates.
- Medicaid fair-hearing rules in
  [42 C.F.R. part 431, subpart E](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-431/subpart-E),
  for covered Medicaid actions, with applicability and procedure verified.
- SNAP fair-hearing rules in
  [7 C.F.R. §273.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/section-273.15),
  for covered SNAP cases/actions, not all state benefits.
- Georgia Administrative Procedure Act, O.C.G.A. title 50, chapter 13: obtain the
  current authoritative text and determine agency/action coverage and exceptions.
- OMB M-25-21: obtain the current official memorandum and status. Treat it as
  federal-agency policy and a **persuasive comparator for state policy**, not a
  direct state obligation merely because it is a federal memorandum.

U.S. federal, state and District of Columbia authority is the primary legal scan
scope. Keep existing EU/foreign references preserved and clearly label them as
non-U.S. comparative/vendor context; do not presume they bind the agency or delete
them from the historical record. A specifically supported foreign applicability
question can be separately referred for agency review.

**Agency action.** Appoint the reviewing official, obtain the relevant primary
texts, and record scoped results or “not yet verified.” Use the new neutral
[06: Horizon scan](../../method/prompts/06-horizon-scan.md) for new work. The
historical scan's “Prompt 03” title and bytes remain unchanged; neutral Prompt 03
continues to mean trigger assessment.
