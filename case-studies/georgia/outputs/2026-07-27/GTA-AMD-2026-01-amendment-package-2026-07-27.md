## How to read this package

**Presentation conventions.** Priority and status are carried by a text label in every instance. In the printed, Word and greyscale versions of this package the label is accompanied by a fill — dark grey for P1, mid grey for P2, light grey for P3 and P4 — and no meaning is carried by fill or colour alone. Every table has a caption and a header row set to repeat across pages. Where a table would have become too wide to read, it has been split into two linked tables sharing an ID column rather than compressed. Plain language precedes legal language in every amendment card. No emoji or symbol carries meaning on its own.

**Redline convention.** Removals are struck through, additions are underlined. Colour highlighting is not used, because it fails in greyscale print and for colour-blind readers.

**One design decision, stated once.** Part 3 requires each card to retain the full proposed text as adoptable wording, and Part 3A requires the same text again as a separate redline entry. Reproducing a provision twice in one package is the drift hazard the Governance Register README warns against — two copies matched by ID diverge on the first correction. Each card therefore carries the complete 3A field set in its lower half, and Part 3A of this package is a conformance index confirming, per amendment, that every 3A field is present and where. Where a redline's proposed column is the complete provision, that column **is** the adoptable text and is not restated. Where the redline shows a fragment of a longer provision, the complete provision follows the redline under **PROPOSED TEXT (complete)**.

**Evidence tiers,** as used in the Governance Register and Anchor Register: E1 verified and established; E2 established but with an inferential step; E3 reported, not independently verified; F1 and F2 forward-looking analysis, legitimate as anticipatory drafting and identified as such wherever relied on.

**Bracketed CHECK markers.** Four locators could not be confirmed at this run and appear as `[CHECK]` with a named confirmer and a pre-adoption deadline. None of them is a placeholder for drafting someone else must finish: in every case the operative text is complete and self-contained, and the CHECK attaches only to a citation that must be verified before publication. This is the treatment required by drafting rules 2.4 and 2.8, which forbid creating an unconfirmed reference and forbid inventing a locator.

## Part 1 — Decision memo

**To:** State Chief Information Officer
**From:** Jordan Loewen, Aspen Consultant
**Date:** 27 July 2026
**Subject:** Amendment package AMD-2026-01 — AI responsible-use instrument family

The July baseline assessment found 35 defects across the seven instruments that govern how state agencies buy and use artificial intelligence. You have accepted all 35. This package converts them into 36 adoptable amendments, together with the contract clauses and the implementation sequence needed to make them bind.

The instruments as they stand share one structural problem. The duties that protect a Georgian from an automated decision sit in the guidelines, where they are written as "should" and cannot be enforced, while the duties that protect the enterprise from a vendor sit in the standard, where they are written as "shall" but have no contract to attach to. The public principles page promises both. This package moves the resident-facing duties into the binding tier, gives the vendor duties a purchasing hook, and leaves the guidelines to explain rather than to carry weight they cannot bear.

Three changes matter more than the rest. First, a new Section 13 of SS-23-002 gives a Georgian affected by an automated determination the right to be told an automated system was used, to be given the reason, and to ask a human being to decide again. Nothing in the family provides this today. It is the state's largest litigation exposure, because a benefits or licensing determination made with automated support and no record of human re-determination is a determination the Attorney General will struggle to defend under the Administrative Procedure Act or under procedural due process. Second, a new Section 14 imposes a default prohibition on agentic operation — AI acting on state systems rather than producing output for review — with a named authorization path. Every other exposure in this package can be remediated after the fact. An agent that has already sent the email, moved the money or changed the record cannot be un-run, and the prohibition costs nothing to impose today. Third, Section 3 and the accompanying clause annex convert roughly forty lines of vendor obligation that currently exist only as policy prose into standard contract terms, and replace an unversioned instruction to follow "NIST guidance" with two named publications and an acceptance criterion. As drafted, a vendor can comply with Section 3 by asserting that it follows NIST; after amendment it must produce a specified artifact or fail evaluation.

Implementation cost is concentrated in four amendments, not spread across thirty-six. Fourteen of the thirty-six need no new resources: they correct text, fix cross-references, or move a duty between tiers, and can be adopted and published without any agency doing anything differently. The disparity-testing provision, the AI inventory, the accessibility conformance duty and the human re-determination workflow are the four that require real capability the state does not currently have, and each is drafted with a phased effective date and a stated interim duty rather than as an immediate obligation the state would breach on day one. Agency burden falls hardest on agencies operating constituent-facing systems, which will need to produce notice text, a re-determination path and an accessibility conformance statement; agencies using generative AI only for internal drafting acquire almost no new work. The dependency that most constrains the schedule is not GTA's: it is the records retention appendix, which requires the Georgia Archives to schedule prompt and output classes, and which no GTA action can accelerate.

Three things this package does not do. It does not resolve whether SS-23-002 took effect on 1 December 2023 or 1 December 2024 — reconciliation item (d), which remains answerable from GTA's own records in minutes and which governs when the existing duties attached. It does not resolve the O.C.G.A. § 50-25-4 subsection divergence, the currency of SS-15-002 and SS-08-045, or the NIST SP 800-63B revision in force; all three are carried as CHECK items with named confirmers and a pre-adoption deadline. And it does not touch the eleven defects listed at Part 7, which were observed while drafting, fall outside the accepted findings, and are routed to the next baseline run rather than drafted here. One of those eleven is material to this package and needs your attention now: the 2025 generative-AI standard carries the number **PS-25-001** in its own header table while every cross-reference in the family, and the Anchor Register, calls it **SS-25-001**. Three amendments in this package amend that instrument. One of the two numbers is wrong, and the package cannot be published until GTA says which.

The adoption path is two tranches. Tranche 1 — the seven P1 amendments and the fourteen that need no new resources — goes to legal review the week of 3 August, to the AI Advisory Council on 2 September if the Council's charter requires it, and to publication for a 1 October 2026 effective date, with the contestability and accessibility provisions phased to 1 January and 26 April 2027 respectively. Tranche 2 — the remaining amendments, all of which depend on tooling, a records schedule or a procurement action — adopts 1 December 2026 with effective dates through 1 July 2027. The first compliance checkpoint falls on 1 February 2027, four months after Tranche 1 takes effect and before any phased date matures, which is the last point at which a defect in this package can be corrected cheaply.

Five items require a decision from you rather than an approval, and are not resolved in the drafting:

1. The disparity-testing provision states a numeric threshold: a selection-rate ratio below 0.80 between the highest and lowest-performing protected-class group, at a minimum cell size of thirty, requires suspension of the use pending remediation. The threshold is a policy choice, not a legal requirement. Do you adopt 0.80, or a different figure?
2. Agentic operation is drafted as a default prohibition with an authorization path. The alternative considered was permission with mandatory logging. Do you prohibit by default, or permit and log?
3. The contestability duty in the new Section 13 attaches to automated systems that **determine** an outcome. It does not attach to systems that only inform a human determination, because attaching it there would reach nearly every use of generative AI in the enterprise. Do you accept that boundary?
4. Independent evaluation is drafted as a duty on you to commission an evaluation on defined triggers using existing contract vehicles, because no evaluation team is funded. The target end state is a standing capability. Do you want the interim duty as drafted, or a budget request instead?
5. Which number is correct for the 2025 generative-AI standard, PS-25-001 or SS-25-001?

I recommend approval of Tranche 1 as drafted, and approval of Tranche 2 subject to the four dependencies named in the implementation plan.

## Part 4 — Contract clause annex

**Proposed as Annex A to SS-23-002, incorporated by §3(d).** Drafted for State Purchasing review. Every vendor-facing duty amended in this package appears here; a duty stated in a policy instrument without a clause is unenforceable, and drafting rule 2.6 requires the clause text rather than the policy statement.

**Placement.** Clauses A.1 to A.7 and A.11 to A.14 belong in the **solicitation** and in the **statewide contract term** for AI products. A.8 to A.10 are **conditional**, included where the product is capable of agentic operation, will process data about identified individuals, or will support a consequential determination. All clauses belong in an **agency-level agreement** where the acquisition is outside a statewide contract.

**A.1 Definitions.** In these clauses, *AI System*, *Generative AI*, *Agentic Operation*, *Consequential Determination* and *AI Incident* have the meanings given in State enterprise standard SS-23-002 as amended, and *State* includes the contracting agency and the Georgia Technology Authority.

**A.2 Responsible AI framework and evidence.** The Contractor shall maintain a written Responsible AI framework and shall provide it to GTA on the Effective Date and within thirty days of any update. The Contractor shall provide, and shall keep current for the term, a written mapping of its practices for the AI System supplied to each of the four functions of NIST AI 100-1 (AI RMF 1.0) — Govern, Map, Measure and Manage — and, where the AI System includes a generative component, to NIST AI 600-1. The mapping shall be accompanied by the system or model documentation relied on, the results of the Contractor's most recent internal evaluation including any adversarial or red-team testing, the results of any third-party assessment, and a statement of known limitations and unsupported uses. Where the Contractor cannot provide an item, it shall state so expressly and give the reason.

**A.3 Data use, retention and model training.** The Contractor shall not retain State data, including prompts, inputs, outputs and metadata, beyond the period necessary to provide the service, and in no case beyond the period stated in the Statement of Work. The Contractor shall not use State data to train, fine-tune, evaluate or otherwise improve any model, and shall not disclose State data to any third party except a subcontractor bound by clause A.13. The Contractor shall, on request, provide a written description of every location and jurisdiction in which State data is processed or stored. On termination the Contractor shall return or destroy State data as the State directs and shall certify the action taken.

**A.4 Accessibility.** Any interface through which a member of the public interacts with the AI System, and any content the AI System generates for publication or service on a member of the public, shall conform to Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 Level AA. The Contractor shall provide an accessibility conformance report identifying the version of the guidelines applied, the evaluation method, and each non-conformance with a remediation date, on the Effective Date and on each material release. The Contractor shall remediate a Level A or Level AA non-conformance within ninety days of notice, or such shorter period as is necessary for the State to meet its obligations under 28 C.F.R. part 35.

**A.5 Explainability and factor disclosure.** Where the AI System supports a Consequential Determination, the Contractor shall provide, in a form the State may lawfully disclose to an affected individual, the principal factors contributing to each output, sufficient for the individual to identify a factual error. The Contractor shall not condition the provision of factor information on the State agreeing to withhold it. Where the Contractor asserts that particular information is a trade secret, it shall identify the information and the ground, and shall provide the principal factors notwithstanding the assertion. The Contractor acknowledges that the State is subject to the Georgia Open Records Act and that the State determines, applying that Act, what may be withheld.

**A.6 Impact assessment and testing artifacts.** The Contractor shall provide an algorithmic impact assessment for the AI System, following the structure of ISO/IEC 42005:2025, stating the assessment method and the contexts in which it was performed, and classifying foreseeable harms as low, medium or high. The Contractor shall cooperate with the State's pre-deployment evaluation under SS-23-002 §9(f), including by providing access to the system as configured for the State, and shall not require the State to rely on the Contractor's own test results in place of that evaluation.

**A.7 Model change notice.** The Contractor shall give the State not less than thirty days' written notice before any change of model, model version, system instruction, retrieval corpus, or the addition or removal of a tool, interface or connector, and shall identify the expected effect on output. Where a change is made without notice, the State may suspend use of the AI System without liability until it has completed a re-evaluation under SS-23-002 §9(g)(iii). Emergency security changes may be made without prior notice and shall be notified within twenty-four hours.

**A.8 Agentic operation.** *(Conditional — include where the product is capable of agentic operation.)* The Contractor shall not enable, and shall not permit to be enabled, any capability of the AI System to take an action falling within Agentic Operation, except as expressly authorised in writing by GTA under SS-23-002 §14. The Contractor shall provide the technical means for the State to disable each such capability independently, to enumerate the actions the AI System is capable of taking, to require human confirmation before an action proceeds, and to halt the AI System within a session. The Contractor shall log every action taken by the AI System with a timestamp and the content or instruction that prompted it, and shall make the log available to the State.

**A.9 Incident notification.** *(Conditional — include where the AI System processes State data or supports a Consequential Determination.)* The Contractor shall notify the State within twenty-four hours of becoming aware of any event that constitutes or may constitute an AI Incident under SS-23-002 §8(c), so as to enable the State to meet its own forty-eight-hour reporting obligation, and shall cooperate in the investigation. The Contractor shall notify the State within twenty-four hours of becoming aware of any unauthorised access to, disclosure of, or use of State data.

**A.10 Disparity testing.** *(Conditional — include where the AI System supports a Consequential Determination.)* The Contractor shall provide the State with the outputs, scores and metadata necessary for the State to perform disparity testing under SS-23-002 §4(e), in a machine-readable form and at no additional charge. The Contractor shall provide the results of any disparity or bias testing it has performed on the AI System, stating the groups tested, the method, the metric and the result. Where the State identifies a disparity exceedance, the Contractor shall cooperate in diagnosis and shall propose remediation within thirty days.

**A.11 Audit and verification.** The State may, at its option and on reasonable notice, authenticate and verify the Contractor's and any subcontractor's application of and adherence to these clauses, including by inspecting documentation and by testing the AI System as configured for the State. The Contractor shall not charge for cooperation with an audit conducted not more than once in a twelve-month period, or following an AI Incident.

**A.12 Records, custody and litigation hold.** The Contractor acknowledges that prompts, outputs, logs, review records and configuration metadata relating to the State's use of the AI System may be public records of the State. The Contractor shall not destroy any such record while a written hold notice from the State is in effect, shall implement a hold within three business days of notice, and shall provide the State with a copy of any such record in a usable format on request and on termination.

**A.13 Subcontractors and flow-down.** The Contractor shall not permit a subcontractor or affiliate to perform any part of the AI System's operation unless that party is bound by terms not less protective than clauses A.2 to A.12. The Contractor shall provide, on the Effective Date and within thirty days of a change, a list of every party that processes State data or contributes a model to the AI System, identifying what each does.

**A.14 Remedies.** Failure to comply with a clause in this Annex is a material breach. Without limiting any other remedy, the State may suspend use of the AI System, withhold payment for the affected service, require remediation within a stated period, and terminate for cause. The State's exercise of a right under SS-23-002 §10 to order termination of use of an AI System is not a breach by the State of this Agreement.

**Note for State Purchasing.** Clause A.5 is the clause most likely to be resisted, because it constrains a vendor's trade-secret position. It is also the clause on which SS-23-002 §13(g) depends: a notice duty the state cannot discharge because its vendor will not supply factor information is a notice duty the state will breach. If A.5 cannot be carried in this form, the alternative is a narrower obligation to provide factor information to the State under confidentiality with an express acknowledgement that the State determines disclosability under the Open Records Act — which is weaker, and should be a recorded decision rather than a drafting concession.
